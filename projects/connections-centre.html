THE CHALLENGE

Useful information should be easier to share.

Communication is not limited to spoken words. A shared visual system can give people and staff more ways to exchange information.

In a busy centre, people may need to communicate personal preferences, find useful information or recognise the people around them. Visual information can support these interactions without assuming that everyone communicates in the same way.

The design focus is practical: make centre information approachable, support staff and client communication, and create resources that can be used in everyday moments.

VISUAL COMMUNICATION SYSTEMRecommended image: 1600 × 1000

THE APPROACH

Build a practical visual language.

Flashcards and clear visual information offer flexible ways to share messages, preferences and useful details.

Resources can help people recognise individual people, communicate what they like, and access centre information. Staff can use the same visual prompts to support a conversation rather than relying only on verbal explanation.

The system is intended to extend communication beyond verbal language while keeping the information relevant, accessible and easy to use.

VISUAL DESIGN

Make the person, not the format, the focus.

Recognisable images and straightforward layouts can help people find information and communicate what matters to them.

Consistent visual cues support familiarity, while the content can reflect individual people, preferences and the practical information needed at the centre.

COMMUNICATION PRINCIPLES

01

02

03

04

Widen the conversation

Support communication beyond verbal language.
